Our Specialized Services Relating to Trademarks

Section 7 Application

Assistance in filing application by Financial Creditors (banks, NBFCs, home buyers, bondholders) to initiate the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against a defaulting company (Corporate Debtor) to recover money or restructure the debt.

Section 9 Application

Assistance in filing application by Operational Creditors (suppliers, service providers, vendors, employees, or government authorities) to initiate the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) in order to recover unpaid dues resulting from goods or services provided.

Section 10 Application

Assistance in filing a distressed company (Corporate Debtor) to voluntarily initiate its own Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process to resolve its liabilities under NCLT protection before creditors take hostile enforcement actions.

Voluntary Liquidation (Section 59 Application)

Assistance in filing a solvent corporate entity to wind up its affairs, pay off its liabilities, and distribute remaining assets to its owners/shareholders smoothly without court litigation.

Submission of Claim Form before the IRP/RP

Following NCLT admission, it refers to formally record, verify, and consolidate all debts owed by the Corporate Debtor to the Insolvency Resolution Professional so that a clear picture of its total liability is established during CIRP or Liquidation.

Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process

To provide a faster, cost-effective, and less disruptive alternative to standard CIRP process specifically for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs). It blends informal out-of-court negotiations with formal judicial approval, allowing the existing promoters to retain management of the business while restructuring debt.

Insolvency of Personal Guarantors to Corporate Debtors

To enable financial creditors (or personal guarantors themselves) to invoke Personal Guarantees executed by promoters/directors when a Corporate Debtor defaults, holding the individual guarantor personally liable to the extent of their personal assets.

Complaints against action of IRP/RP

If the RP's actions are obstructing the CIRP process, mismanaging company’s assets, or displaying bias, stakeholders can move to the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) handling the specific CIRP/Liquidation case.

Supreme Court Trademark Judgements

M/s. Innoventive Industries Ltd. vs. ICICI Bank

SUPREME COURT OF INDIA

This is one of the earliest landmark judgements which dealt with a number of issues related to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Law. One of the Para states that a notice of existence of dispute must be delivered by the Corporate Debtor to the Creditor and the existence of such pre-existing dispute is a sufficient defense against CIRP proceedings.

GHANASHYAM MISHRA AND SONS PRIVATE LIMITED V/s EDELWEISS ASSET RECONSTRUCTION COMPANY LIMITED

SUPREME COURT OF INDIA

Once a Resolution Plan is approved under Section 31(1) of the IBC, it becomes binding on the Corporate Debtor and all stakeholders including creditors, guarantors, and statutory authorities. Any claim including statutory dues or other liabilities that is not part of the approved plan is extinguished when the plan is approved.

TATA STEEL LTD. V/s RAJ KUMAR BANERJEE & ORS.

SUPREME COURT OF INDIA

The court held that the NCLAT does not have the power to allow application for condonation of delay if the appeal is filed beyond the maximum permissible time limit as per Section 61 of the IBC. Held that once the appeal crosses the 45-day outer limit, NCLAT lacks jurisdiction to entertain it.

Design Worx Infrastructure India Pvt. Ltd. V/s Premier Restaurants Private Ltd.

NATIONAL COMPANY LAW APPELLATE TRIBUNAL

The court held that if sufficient evidence is provided as to the existence of a pre-existing dispute between the parties prior to the issuance of notice U/s Section 8 of the IBC, then, CIRP proceedings U/s 9 of IBC cannot be initiated.

Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel v Satish Kumar Gupta

SUPREME COURT OF INDIA

The Supreme Court held that the commercial decisions regarding the distribution of funds, viability, and operational setup of the Corporate Debtor belong exclusively to the CoC. Judicial review by the NCLT or NCLAT is strictly restricted to checking compliance with Section 30(2) of the IBC. Adjudicating authorities cannot trespass into or modify the commercial distribution determined by the CoC.

P. Mohanraj vs M/S. Shah Brothers Ispat Pvt. Ltd.

SUPREME COURT OF INDIA

Section 138 (cheque bounce) proceedings against the Corporate Debtor company were stayed/stopped during the moratorium period under Section 14 of the IBC. The Court held that Section 138 actions are quasi-criminal in nature and aimed at money recovery, which would deplete corporate assets during CIRP. Section 138 read with Section 141 NI Act proceedings against the directors, signatories, and natural persons responsible for the company were NOT stopped. The Section 14 moratorium protects only the Corporate Debtor entity, so personal criminal liability of directors continues independently.
